# Overview

The Famine of Jhal was a devastating ecological collapse that struck the south-eastern reaches of [[Cenora]] in the decades immediately following the [[War of Laws]]. Faced with the total starvation of their people, the region's desperate rulers embraced necromancy to substitute a dying living workforce with an eternal undead one. This event marks the ideological and practical founding of the Jhalian [[Necrocracy]].

# Prelude & Causes

While the region escaped the sheer geographical destruction seen in the [[God-Scar Strait]], the lingering cosmic friction from the divine conflict severely corrupted the local climate. Searing temperatures and supernatural blights ravaged the arable land. Most forms of agriculture became entirely impossible to sustain, and the kingdom's mortal population began dying of heat exhaustion and starvation. The living monarch - later known as [[The Undying Sovereign]] - was forced to seek drastic alternatives to prevent the extinction of their kingdom.

# The Event

Realising that mortal bodies were too frail to work the cursed surface, the monarch turned to the canonical magic of [[Cavera, Goddess of Death]]. In a massive, state-sponsored ritual, the first generation of the kingdom's dead were animated. These skeletal and zombie labourers, immune to the blistering sun and requiring no sustenance, were sent into the fields and strip mines to tend failing crops and gather resources.

Simultaneously, gripped by the terror of their own mortality, the monarch secretly contacted fleeing heretical Runesmiths. While publicly praising Cavera, they bound their own soul to a massive subterranean vault using [[Traditions of Dismagic|Relic Dismagic]], becoming a Lich to ensure they could oversee their kingdom for eternity.

# Aftermath & Legacy

This drastic measure was highly controversial amongst neighbouring kingdoms, and even among some of the city's own people. The undead workforce yielded enough food and alchemical reagents to save the surviving populace, allowing the living to retreat into the cool limestone caverns below the surface. Over the next century, this temporary survival measure codified into a deeply structured, bizarre utopia where the living pursue philosophy and art, entirely subsidised by the labour of their ancestors.
